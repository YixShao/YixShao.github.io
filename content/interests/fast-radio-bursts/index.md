Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are brief, bright flashes of coherent radio emission, usually lasting only milliseconds and arriving from extragalactic distances. Some sources have produced only one detected burst, while others repeat. Their short durations, enormous apparent luminosities, and diverse host environments make them powerful laboratories for studying compact objects and energetic plasma processes.

The frequency-dependent delay of an FRB records the free electrons encountered along its path. With a host-galaxy distance, this dispersion can therefore probe otherwise difficult-to-detect matter between galaxies. The discovery of an FRB-like burst from the Galactic magnetar SGR 1935+2154 established that magnetars can produce at least some FRBs, although the full diversity of sources and emission mechanisms remains an open question.

## Why they interest me

FRBs connect radio time-domain astronomy to neutron-star activity, high-energy transients, and their environments. I am particularly interested in their connection with pulsars and also curious about where are they actually coming from.
